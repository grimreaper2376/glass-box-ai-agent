Calm drift — small steady gains, low volatility: an ordinary quiet market, the baseline every other scenario is measured against. Trending bull — sustained upward moves at moderate volatility: tests whether risk controls needlessly hold the system back during a genuine rally. Grinding bear — sustained decline at elevated volatility: tests whether losses are cut early rather than allowed to compound. Violent chop — no net direction at all, but sharp swings in both directions: tests whether the system avoids getting whipsawed into repeated small losses. Cascading crash — a severe, fast decline at extreme volatility: tests whether the Guardian actually protects capital when it matters most.

Narrative strength A theme is only tradable while attention is still building and price has not caught up.
What this is for. In crypto, price often follows a story — a theme the market gets excited about (AI agents, real-world assets, DePIN). This panel tracks how much attention each theme is getting and, crucially, whether that attention is rising faster than price has already moved. A theme that is being talked about more while its coins haven't run yet is an early signal; one that's loud only after a big rally is just news. The scout surfaces the first kind and ignores the second. It never opens a trade on its own — it's a radar for where momentum may be forming, and like everything else it's still gated by the Constitution and Guardian before any position is taken.

This is a different tool from Control Center's stress test, not a duplicate of it. This tab replays what actually happened on Binance over a real historical window — real prices, real volatility, real market character, whatever that character was. Control Center's stress test instead runs five synthetic, adversarial scripts (a scripted crash, a scripted grind down) specifically designed to probe one behaviour each. Use this tab to ask "how would the system have performed in reality"; use Control Center's stress test to ask "does the Guardian actually intervene when a crash is scripted to happen."
